Frequency measures how often something repeats per second. It appears everywhere in modern life — from the processor speed of your computer to the radio stations you listen to, from musical notes to WiFi signals.

## What is Frequency?

Frequency is measured in Hertz (Hz), where 1 Hz means one cycle per second. The unit is named after Heinrich Hertz, who first proved the existence of electromagnetic waves.

## Frequency Scale

- 1 Hz = 1 cycle per second
- 1 kHz (kilohertz) = 1,000 Hz
- 1 MHz (megahertz) = 1,000,000 Hz
- 1 GHz (gigahertz) = 1,000,000,000 Hz
- 1 THz (terahertz) = 1,000,000,000,000 Hz

## Everyday Frequency Examples

### Music and Sound
- Human hearing range: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)
- Middle C on piano: 261.6 Hz
- Concert A (tuning standard): 440 Hz
- Bass guitar lowest note: ~41 Hz

### Radio and Communication
- AM radio: 530 kHz - 1,700 kHz
- FM radio: 87.5 MHz - 108 MHz
- WiFi 2.4 GHz band: 2,400 MHz
- WiFi 5 GHz band: 5,000 MHz
- 5G cellular: 600 MHz - 39 GHz

### Computing
- Modern CPU clock speed: 3-5 GHz
- RAM frequency: 3,200 MHz - 7,200 MHz
- USB data transfer rates are also measured in MHz/GHz

### Power Systems
- US electrical grid: 60 Hz
- European electrical grid: 50 Hz
- This is why some electronics need adapters when traveling internationally — it is not just about voltage, but frequency too.
