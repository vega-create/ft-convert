Accurate measurements are the foundation of successful cooking and baking. Whether you are following a recipe from another country or scaling a recipe up or down, understanding measurement conversions is essential.

## Volume Conversions for Cooking

The most common cooking measurements in American recipes are cups, tablespoons, and teaspoons. Here is how they relate to each other and to metric measurements:

- 1 cup = 16 tablespoons = 48 teaspoons = 236.6 mL
- 1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons = 14.8 mL
- 1 teaspoon = 4.9 mL
- 1 fluid ounce = 2 tablespoons = 29.6 mL

## Weight vs Volume

Many professional bakers prefer measuring by weight rather than volume because it is more accurate. A cup of flour can vary significantly depending on how it is scooped, but 120 grams of flour is always 120 grams.

## Common Ingredient Weights per Cup

Here are approximate weights for common baking ingredients:

- All-purpose flour: 1 cup = 120g
- Granulated sugar: 1 cup = 200g
- Brown sugar (packed): 1 cup = 220g
- Butter: 1 cup = 227g (2 sticks)
- Milk: 1 cup = 245g
- Honey: 1 cup = 340g

## International Differences

Be aware that cup sizes differ between countries:

- US cup = 236.6 mL
- UK cup = 284.1 mL
- Australian cup = 250 mL
- Japanese cup = 200 mL

Always check which measurement standard a recipe uses before converting.

## Tips for Accurate Measurement

1. Use the correct measuring tools (dry cups for dry ingredients, liquid measuring cups for liquids)
2. Level off dry ingredients with a straight edge
3. Read liquid measurements at eye level
4. When in doubt, weigh ingredients on a kitchen scale
